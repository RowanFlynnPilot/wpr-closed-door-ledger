The Closed Door Ledger

Methodology

How the ledger finds closed-session notices, what it records from them, and what its counts don't say.

What the counts mean

Every count is of closed sessions noticed in a body's online agenda.

  • A posted agenda isn't necessarily the legal notice. Under Wis. Stat. § 19.84(1), a body can also give notice by physical posting and by notifying the news media.
  • A session that was noticed may never have been held.
  • Coverage is the bodies listed in bodies.yaml, from January 1, 2024. Nothing outside them is covered. Marathon County is not yet included: its site refuses automated requests.
  • Each committee, commission and board is its own governmental body under s. 19.82(1), so each is reported on its own. A few bodies (task forces, intergovernmental police and fire bodies, a county commission posted on the city's site) are archived but not shown until an editor settles how to report them.

How notices are read

  • The ledger reads each body's agenda from the platform it publishes on: CivicClerk (City of Wausau), BoardBook (both school districts), AgendaCenter (Weston) and Municode (Kronenwetter). Every distinct version of every agenda is archived, with the time it was first seen.
  • An agenda is split into its numbered items. A closed-session item is one under a heading or motion to convene in closed session that cites s. 19.85(1) or s. 19.86. A closed session noticed without any citation is recorded too, as one item with no statute cited.
  • Items come from the latest version of the agenda seen before the meeting began; for meetings before the ledger started, from the earliest version it archived.
  • Weston posts each agenda as part of the full meeting packet. The ledger reads only the agenda at the front of it.
  • Scanned agendas are read with open-source OCR (Tesseract), and every item read that way is checked by an editor.
  • No generative AI reads or classifies notices. The rules are deterministic and tested against real notices; the code is on GitHub.

What each item records

item_textThe notice's exact wording.
citation_rawThe citation as the notice writes it, typos included ("19.85(1)(g for", "19.85(d)").
exemptionsThe s. 19.85(1) paragraph letters cited. An item citing "19.85(d)" without "(1)" is read as (d).
other_citationsAny other statute cited, such as s. 19.86 (bargaining) or s. 118.125 (student records).
purpose_textThe subject the notice names in its own words, apart from the statute's wording. Empty when the notice only restates the statute.
notice_url, archive_urlThe agenda on the body's site, and the ledger's archived copy.

Sessions under s. 19.86 alone are collective bargaining negotiations, to which the open meetings law does not apply. They are counted apart and never in the exemption mix.

The law, briefly

  • A notice must give the subject matter of any contemplated closed session, in a form reasonably likely to inform the public (s. 19.84(2)). How specific it must be is a case-by-case test (State ex rel. Buswell v. Tomah Area School District, 2007 WI 71).
  • Before voting to close, the presiding officer must announce the business and the specific exemption, and each member's vote on the motion is recorded (s. 19.85(1), s. 19.88(3)).
  • To reopen in open session within 12 hours, the body must say so in the original notice (s. 19.85(2)).

Editorial policy

  • The ledger states what a notice says and what it cites. Nothing more.
  • Nothing is characterized automatically. Every published flag is an editor's decision, with a note, made after the body was asked for comment.
  • Minutes name private people, such as claimants. The ledger never copies those names; it links to the source.
